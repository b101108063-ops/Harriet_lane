I. TRAUMA ASSESSMENT#

Primary Survey: ABCDE#

Letter Component Key Actions
A Airway Assess patency, neurologic status, C-spine; call anesthesia/intensivist for difficult airway
B Breathing Check for pneumo/hemothorax, optimize BVM ventilation, confirm EtCO₂
C Circulation Control hemorrhage, assess shock (HR, BP, cap refill, pulses), place 2 large-bore IVs
D Disability GCS, pupil exam, mechanism of injury
E Exposure Full skin exam, remove clothing, check for overdose

Secondary Survey#

Region Key Findings
HEAD Scalp laceration/hematoma, skull depression, raccoon eyes, Battle sign, GCS
FACE Facial bone tenderness, nasal septum hematoma, dental injury
EYES Pupil size/symmetry, hyphema, globe rupture
CHEST Clavicle deformity, asymmetric breath sounds, flail chest, rib fractures
ABDOMEN Tenderness, distention, seatbelt sign, flank ecchymoses, LUQ rib tenderness
PELVIS Instability → place pelvic binder
NECK C-spine tenderness, tracheal deviation, distended neck veins
BACK C-spine stabilization, log-roll, vertebral step-offs
SKIN Cap refill, lacerations, contusions
EXTREMITIES Deformity, open fracture, neurovascular status, compartment syndrome signs

Initial Labs#

Test Purpose
Hemoglobin, blood gas, lactate, glucose, electrolytes Critical care assessment
Coagulation (PT, aPTT, INR, fibrinogen) Operative planning
Type and screen/cross Anticipated surgery
Lactate Poor tissue perfusion, internal hemorrhage
Urinalysis Hematuria → kidney/urinary tract injury
CBC Note: Initial Hgb can be normal in acute hemorrhage
Pregnancy test All females of child-bearing age

II. HEAD AND NECK TRAUMA#

PECARN Algorithm for CT Head Imaging#

Validated pediatric decision aid predicting need for CT after head injury Risk: CT radiation causes lethal malignancy — higher risk in children! Only applies to GCS ≥14

Children <2 years:

Finding Action Risk of ciTBI
GCS ≤14 OR altered mental status OR palpable skull fracture CT 4.4%
Scalp hematoma (non-frontal) OR LOC ≥5 sec OR severe mechanism Consider CT 0.9%
No concerning findings Observe <0.02%

Children ≥2 years:

Finding Action Risk of ciTBI
GCS ≤14 OR basilar skull fracture OR altered mental status CT 4.3%
Vomiting OR LOC OR severe headache OR severe mechanism Consider CT 0.9%
No concerning findings Observe <0.05%

ciTBI = clinically important traumatic brain injury

C-Spine Clearance#

PECARN Criteria — obtain imaging if ANY present:

  • Altered mental status
  • Focal neurologic deficits
  • Complaint of neck pain
  • Torticollis
  • Substantial injury to torso
  • Predisposing condition
  • High-risk MVA
  • Diving accident

NEXUS Criteria — imaging if ANY present:

  • Midline cervical tenderness
  • Altered level of alertness
  • Evidence of intoxication
  • Neurologic deficit
  • Painful distracting injury

SCIWORA#

Spinal Cord Injury Without Radiographic Abnormality

  • 30–40% of children with traumatic myelopathy
  • Most common in children ≤8 years
  • MRI should be considered

III. CONCUSSION#

Feature Details
Definition Neurometabolic dysfunction (not structural) after head trauma
Neuroimaging Usually normal
Resolution Most symptoms resolve in 1–3 weeks
Prolonged symptoms >3 weeks → specialist referral

Concussion Symptoms#

Category Symptoms
Physical Headache, photophobia, phonophobia
Cognitive Feeling foggy, fatigue, difficulty focusing, forgetfulness
Emotional Irritability, sadness
Sleep Drowsiness, sleeping more/less

Return-to-Play (6 Steps)#

Step Activity
1 Back to regular activities
2 Light aerobic activity (5–10 min walking/jogging)
3 Moderate activity (moderate jogging, weightlifting)
4 Heavy noncontact activity (sprinting, drills)
5 Full contact in controlled practice
6 Full competition/game play

⚠️ Only progress if no new/worsening symptoms. Steps take days to months.

IV. THORACIC, ABDOMINAL, AND PELVIC TRAUMA#

Life-Threatening Findings#

Condition Signs Emergent Treatment
Tension pneumothorax Respiratory distress, unequal breath sounds, hypotension Immediate needle decompression, chest tube
Massive hemothorax Hypotension, tachycardia, dullness Chest tube, volume resuscitation
Cardiac tamponade Muffled heart sounds, hypotension, pulsus paradoxus Pericardiocentesis
Aortic dissection Severe chest/abdominal pain, pulse differential Stable: β-blocker; Unstable: OR

Rib Fractures & Flail Chest#

Finding Implication
Multiple rib fractures in <3 years Consider child abuse
Flail chest Intubate + positive pressure ventilation
Pulmonary contusion WITHOUT rib fracture More common in children (compliant chest wall)

Seatbelt Sign#

Bruising in seatbelt distribution — sensitivity 70.6%, specificity 82.4% for surgical abdominal injury

PECARN Blunt Abdominal Trauma Rule#

Very low risk for IAI (99% sensitivity) — ALL must be absent:

  • No abdominal wall trauma / seatbelt sign
  • GCS >13
  • No abdominal tenderness
  • No thoracic wall trauma
  • No abdominal pain
  • No decreased breath sounds
  • No vomiting

FAST Exam#

Result Interpretation
Positive IAI likely → CT warranted
Negative Cannot rule out → CT or serial FAST

Pooled sensitivity: 35%, Specificity: 96%

VI. DENTAL TRAUMA#

Primary vs Permanent Teeth#

Feature Primary Permanent
Age 6 months – 3 years 6–21 years
Roots Shorter Longer
Biting surface Smooth Ridged

Avulsion#

Tooth Type Action
Primary Do NOT reimplant — outpatient dental follow-up
Permanent EMERGENCY — reimplant within 60 minutes

Storage media (if reimplantation not possible): Milk > Hank’s balanced salt solution > saliva > saline

Tooth Fracture Types#

Type Follow-Up
Enamel fracture Dental evaluation for possible bonding
Enamel-dentin fracture Dental evaluation in 48–72 hr
Enamel-dentin-pulp fracture Immediate dental evaluation
Alveolar ridge fracture Emergent dental evaluation

VII. OPHTHALMOLOGIC TRAUMA#

Chemical Eye Injury#

Step Action
1 IMMEDIATE irrigation with water/saline/LR
2 Call Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222
3 Determine acid (<7) or alkali (>7.4) — Alkali more damaging!
4 Continue irrigation ≥20 minutes or until pH 7.0–7.4

Ruptured Globe#

⚠️ EMERGENT ophthalmology consultation

  • Stop exam, place rigid eye shield
  • Do NOT instill eye drops
  • Elevate HOB
  • Control symptoms (antiemetics, pain control)
  • Antibiotics

Corneal Abrasion#

Feature Details
Symptoms Red eye, tearing, intense pain, photophobia, FB sensation
Diagnosis Fluorescein staining with Wood’s lamp
Treatment Artificial tears, antibiotic ointment, analgesics

Indication Treatment

Human bite Amoxicillin/clavulanate 5 days

Dog bite infection median: 24 hours | Cat bite infection median: 12 hours

Closure Decision#

Location Recommendation
Face Primary closure acceptable (<24 hr)
Other areas Avoid primary closure (<12 hr)
All Do NOT close with tissue adhesive

IX. BURNS#

Burn Classification#

TABLE 2.5 — BURN DEPTH

Depth Layer Clinical Findings
Superficial Epidermis Dry, painful, erythematous (like sunburn)
Partial thickness Dermis Moist, painful, blisters, blanching
Full thickness Subcutaneous/fascia/muscle/bone Pale, charred, waxy, leathery, insensate

Parkland Formula#

4 mL/kg × %TBSA burned over first 24 hours

  • First half: first 8 hours
  • Second half: next 16 hours

Goal urine output: 0.5–1 mL/kg/hr

TBSA Estimation#

  • Patient’s palm + adducted fingers ≈ 1% TBSA
  • Use modified Lund-Browder chart for children

Fluid Resuscitation by TBSA#

TBSA Action
<15% Consider IV fluids based on clinical status
≥15% LR or NS per Parkland formula

Burn Center Transfer Indications#

⚠️ Partial-thickness ≥10% TBSA | Full-thickness any | Face/hands/feet/genitalia | Electrical/chemical | Inhalation | Burns + trauma

X. NONACCIDENTIC TRAUMA (CHILD ABUSE)#

Red Flags in History#

Red Flag Examples
Inconsistent history Does not match injury pattern/severity
Inconsistent with development Child cannot yet walk but has fall injury
Vague/changing explanations Different stories from different people
Delay in presentation —

Physical Exam Red Flags#

Finding Pattern
Bruises Protected areas, multiple, various healing stages, unusual locations
Burns Sharply demarcated edges, object shapes, cigarette burns, stocking/glove distribution
Other Frenulum tears, loop marks, bites

Abusive Fractures (High Specificity)#

  • Posterior rib fractures
  • Bucket handle / metaphyseal corner fractures
  • Spine and scapula fractures
  • Skull fractures (with other findings)

Imaging#

Age Study
<2 years Full skeletal survey
2–5 years Clinical judgment
>5 years Targeted imaging

Reporting#

⚠️ ALL healthcare providers are mandated by law to report suspected child maltreatment.

XI. SEXUAL ABUSE#

Indications for Urgent Evaluation#

  • Abuse within 72–96 hours
  • Genital/anal injuries requiring treatment
  • Obvious forensic evidence
  • Danger of continued abuse
  • Suicidal/homicidal ideation

STI Testing in Sexual Abuse#

Population Testing
Adolescents Recommended for all
Prepubertal Consider if: penetration, stranger abuse, known infected perpetrator, sibling with STI, signs/symptoms
