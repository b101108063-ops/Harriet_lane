## I. NEUROLOGIC EXAMINATION

### A. Mental Status

- <span style="color:#60a5fa">Infants</span>: Observe "cuteness" and ability to dynamically engage caretakers
- <span style="color:#60a5fa">Toddlers</span>: Bring toys; observe and engage in play
- <span style="color:#60a5fa">School age</span>: Ask children to draw or describe school or friends

### B. Cranial Nerves

| Cranial Nerve | Function | Test |
|---------------|----------|------|
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">I. Olfactory</span> | Smell | Observe response to odors |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">II. Optic</span> | Vision | Visual acuity, fundus, red reflex |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">III. Oculomotor</span> | Pupillary reflex, eye movements, eyelid elevation | Pupil response, extraocular movements |

| <span style="color:#60a5fa">VI. Abducens</span> | Lateral gaze | Eye movements |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">VII. Facial</span> | Facial movement | Symmetry at rest and with emotive activation |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">VIII. Vestibulocochlear</span> | Hearing, balance | Finger rub, localize sound |

### C. Motor

#### 1. Muscle bulk: <span style="color:#f87171">Atrophy or pseudohypertrophy</span> is a red flag.

#### 2. Tone: <span style="color:#60a5fa">Spasticity</span>, rigidity, hypotonia

#### 3. Strength rating scale:

| Grade | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 0/5 | No movement |
| 1/5 | Flicker of movement |
| 2/5 | Movement against gravity only |
| 3/5 | Movement against gravity, not resistance |
| 4/5 | Subnormal strength against resistance |
| <span style="color:#34d399">5/5</span> | Normal strength |

#### 4. <span style="color:#f87171">Red flags</span>: Scissoring, toe-walking, inability to supinate hand, clasped thumb

### D. Sensory

#### 1. Primarily important if concern for spinal cord defect or peripheral nerve injury

#### 2. Focus on distal deficit with preserved proximal = polyneuropathy

## II. NEUROLOGIC EMERGENCIES

### A. Stroke

#### 1. Presentation and etiology:

*a. <span style="color:#f87171">50% ischemic, 50% hemorrhagic</span> overall in pediatric stroke*

*b. Approximately <span style="color:#f87171">80% ischemic</span> in perinatal stroke*

*c. Stroke mimics are more common than stroke itself (migraine, seizure,

functional disorders, mass lesions)

#### 2. Etiologies vary by age:

| Perinatal AIS | Childhood AIS | Cerebral Venous Thrombosis |
|---------------|---------------|---------------------------|
| Not fully understood | Cardiac disease, cerebral arteriopathy | Inherited thrombophilia |
| Combination of maternal and fetal factors | Hypercoagulable states | Drugs, infections |
| | Sickle cell disease | Dehydration, nephrotic syndrome |

#### 3. Management:

*a. Stroke team activation or urgent neurology consultation*
*b. Supportive care: Maintain normoglycemia, normothermia, oxygenation*
*c. Reperfusion therapies: IV tPA or mechanical thrombectomy may be

considered in appropriate circumstances

*d. Children with <span style="color:#60a5fa">sickle cell disease</span>: Hydration and emergent exchange

transfusion

### B. Status Epilepticus and Breakthrough Seizures

#### 1. Definition: <span style="color:#f87171">Continuous seizure activity ≥5 minutes</span> or two or more

discrete seizures without return of consciousness between them.

#### 2. Breakthrough seizures: Assess for missed medications, lack of sleep,

stress, drugs/alcohol, illness, dehydration, drug interactions.

### C. Encephalopathy

#### 1. Definitions:

*a. <span style="color:#60a5fa">Encephalopathy</span>: Diffuse neuronal dysfunction manifesting as acute

or chronic altered mental status

*b. <span style="color:#60a5fa">Encephalitis</span>: Inflammation of brain parenchyma secondary to

infection or inflammatory response

#### 2. Selected causes of encephalopathy:

- Infectious: meningitis, encephalitis, ADEM
- Autoimmune: NMDAR, VGKC-complex, Hashimoto's
- Trauma, toxins, metabolic disorders
- <span style="color:#f87171">Hypertension</span>, hypoxic-ischemic injury

### D. Hydrocephalus with Increased Intracranial Pressure

#### 1. Clinical signs: Macrocephaly, increasing head circumference, bulging

fontanelle, headaches, vomiting, <span style="color:#f87171">Cushing's triad</span> (hypertension,
bradycardia, irregular respirations), papilledema

#### 2. Treatment:

*a. Medical: <span style="color:#fb923c">Acetazolamide</span> and <span style="color:#fb923c">furosemide</span> may decrease CSF production*
*b. Surgical: CSF shunting vs. endoscopic third ventriculostomy (ETV)*

### E. Weakness

#### 1. <span style="color:#60a5fa">Botulism</span>

*a. Etiology: <span style="color:#c084fc">Clostridium botulinum</span> toxin irreversibly cleaves protein*

complex necessary for acetylcholine vesicle release

*b. Clinical features: Subacute onset weakness concentrating in eye, face,

and bulbar muscles early. Dilated and poorly reactive pupils.

*c. Treatment: <span style="color:#fb923c">Human botulism immune globulin (BabyBIG)</span>; Airway*

management; ~25% require intubation

#### 2. <span style="color:#60a5fa">Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS)</span>

*a. Etiology: Immune attack against peripheral nerve myelin;

triggered by <span style="color:#c084fc">Campylobacter jejuni</span>, Zika, SARS-CoV-2

*b. Clinical features: <span style="color:#f87171">Ascending weakness</span> and sensory loss with rapid

decline; ~20% require mechanical ventilation

*c. Diagnosis: <span style="color:#f87171">Elevated spinal fluid protein without cellular infiltrate</span>

("albuminocytologic dissociation") in 50–70%

*d. Treatment: <span style="color:#fb923c">IVIG</span> (0.4 g/kg daily for 5 days) or plasmapheresis*

### F. Ataxia

#### 1. Definition: Impaired coordination of movement and balance; broad-based gait

#### 2. Differential diagnosis of acute ataxia:

- Ingestion (antiseizure meds, alcohol, heavy metals)
- Postinfectious: acute cerebellar ataxia, cerebellitis
- Head trauma: cerebellar contusion or hemorrhage
- Basilar migraine, benign paroxysmal vertigo
- <span style="color:#60a5fa">Opsoclonus myoclonus ataxia syndrome</span>: Consider <span style="color:#c084fc">neuroblastoma</span>
- GBS or Miller-Fisher variant

## III. CHRONIC OR GRADUALLY PROGRESSIVE CONDITIONS

### A. Headaches

#### 1. Classification:

*a. <span style="color:#60a5fa">Primary headaches</span>: Migraine, tension type, cluster, TACs*
*b. <span style="color:#60a5fa">Secondary headaches</span>: Trauma, infection, vascular disorder,

increased intracranial pressure

#### 2. Migraine headache:

*a. Can be throbbing, pulsatile, or pressure-like*
*b. Usually bifrontal in children, unilateral in adolescents/adults*
*c. Many potential triggers (stress, caffeine, menses, sleep disruption)

#### 3. Treatment:

*a. Acute: NSAIDs (<span style="color:#fb923c">naproxen</span>, <span style="color:#fb923c">ibuprofen</span>), <span style="color:#fb923c">acetaminophen</span>;

<span style="color:#fb923c">Triptans</span> at migraine onset; Avoid medication overuse

*b. Preventive: <span style="color:#fb923c">Propranolol</span>, <span style="color:#fb923c">Amitriptyline</span>, <span style="color:#fb923c">Topiramate</span>*

Lifestyle modification: adequate sleep, meals, hydration, regular
exercise, weight loss if overweight

### B. Seizures

#### 1. Definition: Paroxysmal, transient, synchronized discharge of cortical

neurons resulting in alteration of function

#### 2. Febrile seizures:

*a. <span style="color:#60a5fa">Simple febrile seizure</span>: Primary generalized, 6–60 months, nonfocal,

<span style="color:#34d399"><15 minutes</span>, does not recur in 24 hours
**(1) Management: Identify source of fever; No neuroimaging, EEG, or**

bloodwork needed for simple febrile seizure in well-appearing,
fully immunized child

*b. <span style="color:#60a5fa">Complex febrile seizure</span>: Focal, >15 minutes, or recurs within 24 hours*

#### 3. Treatment of unprovoked seizure:

*a. First seizure, nonfocal, return to baseline: Usually no antiseizure

medication indicated; ~50% recurrence in 2–5 years

*b. Pharmacotherapy: Choose according to seizure type (see Table 20.2)

#### 4. Selected epilepsy syndromes:

| Syndrome | Evaluation | Treatment | Comments |
|----------|------------|-----------|----------|
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Infantile spasms</span> | EEG with hypsarrhythmia; MRI | <span style="color:#fb923c">High-dose steroids</span> or <span style="color:#fb923c">ACTH</span>; <span style="color:#fb923c">Vigabatrin</span> | Onset 2+ months; peak 4–6 months |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Childhood absence seizures</span> | EEG with 3–4 Hz spike-wave | <span style="color:#fb923c">Ethosuximide</span>, <span style="color:#fb923c">lamotrigine</span>, <span style="color:#fb923c">valproic acid</span> | Hyperventilation precipitates seizure |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Dravet syndrome</span> | Genetic testing (SCN1A mutation) | <span style="color:#fb923c">Clobazam</span>, <span style="color:#fb923c">levetiracetam</span>, <span style="color:#fb923c">valproate</span>, <span style="color:#fb923c">cannabidiol</span> | Seizures starting in infancy, often associated with heat |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Juvenile myoclonic epilepsy</span> | Clinical history; sleep-deprived EEG | <span style="color:#fb923c">Lamotrigine</span>, <span style="color:#fb923c">levetiracetam</span>, <span style="color:#fb923c">valproate</span> | Adolescent onset; myoclonus upon awakening |

### C. Neuromuscular Disorders

#### 1. <span style="color:#60a5fa">Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA)</span>

*a. Etiology: Autosomal recessive mutations in SMN1 gene; Severity*

correlates with SMN2 copy number

*b. Treatment: <span style="color:#fb923c">Nusinersen</span> (Spinraza), <span style="color:#fb923c">Onasemnogene abeparvovec</span>

(Zolgensma), <span style="color:#fb923c">Risdiplam</span> (Evrysdi)

#### 2. <span style="color:#60a5fa">Duchenne/Becker muscular dystrophies (DMD/BMD)</span>

*a. Etiology: X-linked mutation in DMD gene encoding dystrophin*
*b. Treatment: <span style="color:#fb923c">Corticosteroids</span> (prednisone, deflazacort); <span style="color:#fb923c">Exon-skipping</span>

therapies (eteplirsen, viltolarsen, golodirsen, casimersen)

#### 3. <span style="color:#60a5fa">Myasthenia gravis</span>

*a. Etiology: Autoantibodies binding AChR or MuSK*
*b. Clinical features: <span style="color:#f87171">Fatigable weakness</span>; Orbital muscles (ptosis,

diplopia); Bulbar weakness (slurred/nasal voice, difficulty chewing,
swallowing)

*c. Treatment:*

**(1) Myasthenic crisis: <span style="color:#fb923c">Plasmapheresis</span>, <span style="color:#fb923c">IVIG</span>, <span style="color:#fb923c">IV neostigmine</span>**
**(2) Chronic: <span style="color:#fb923c">Pyridostigmine</span>, <span style="color:#fb923c">prednisone</span>,

<span style="color:#fb923c">immunosuppressants</span> (azathioprine, mycophenolate, rituximab)

### D. Functional Neurologic Disorders (FNDs)

#### 1. Definition: Symptoms of altered voluntary motor or sensory function

not explained by recognized neurologic or medical conditions

#### 2. Diagnosis:

*a. <span style="color:#60a5fa">Hoover's sign</span>: Weakness of hip extension returns to normal with*

contralateral hip flexion against resistance

*b. Breakaway/give-way weakness*
*c. Variability in frequency of tremor*

#### 3. Management:

*a. Physical therapy rehabilitation and cognitive behavioral therapy*
