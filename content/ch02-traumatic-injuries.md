## I. TRAUMA ASSESSMENT

### Primary Survey: ABCDE

| Letter | Component | Key Actions |
|--------|-----------|------------|
| **A** | <span style="color:#22d3ee">Airway</span> | Assess patency, neurologic status, C-spine; call anesthesia/intensivist for difficult airway |
| **B** | <span style="color:#22d3ee">Breathing</span> | Check for pneumo/hemothorax, optimize BVM ventilation, confirm EtCO₂ |
| **C** | <span style="color:#22d3ee">Circulation</span> | Control hemorrhage, assess shock (HR, BP, cap refill, pulses), place 2 large-bore IVs |
| **D** | <span style="color:#22d3ee">Disability</span> | GCS, pupil exam, mechanism of injury |
| **E** | <span style="color:#22d3ee">Exposure</span> | Full skin exam, remove clothing, check for overdose |

### Secondary Survey

| Region | Key Findings |
|--------|-------------|
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">HEAD</span> | Scalp laceration/hematoma, skull depression, raccoon eyes, Battle sign, GCS |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">FACE</span> | Facial bone tenderness, nasal septum hematoma, dental injury |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">EYES</span> | Pupil size/symmetry, hyphema, globe rupture |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">CHEST</span> | Clavicle deformity, asymmetric breath sounds, flail chest, rib fractures |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">ABDOMEN</span> | Tenderness, distention, seatbelt sign, flank ecchymoses, LUQ rib tenderness |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">PELVIS</span> | Instability → place pelvic binder |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">NECK</span> | C-spine tenderness, tracheal deviation, distended neck veins |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">BACK</span> | C-spine stabilization, log-roll, vertebral step-offs |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">SKIN</span> | Cap refill, lacerations, contusions |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">EXTREMITIES</span> | Deformity, open fracture, neurovascular status, compartment syndrome signs |

### Initial Labs

| Test | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Hemoglobin</span>, blood gas, lactate, glucose, electrolytes | Critical care assessment |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Coagulation</span> (PT, aPTT, INR, fibrinogen) | Operative planning |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Type and screen/cross</span> | Anticipated surgery |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Lactate</span> | Poor tissue perfusion, internal hemorrhage |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Urinalysis</span> | Hematuria → kidney/urinary tract injury |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">CBC</span> | Note: Initial Hgb can be <span style="color:#34d399">normal</span> in acute hemorrhage |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Pregnancy test</span> | All females of child-bearing age |

## II. HEAD AND NECK TRAUMA

### PECARN Algorithm for CT Head Imaging

> <span style="color:#60a5fa">Validated pediatric decision aid</span> predicting need for CT after head injury
> Risk: <span style="color:#f87171">CT radiation causes lethal malignancy — higher risk in children!</span>
> Only applies to <span style="color:#60a5fa">GCS ≥14</span>

**Children <2 years:**

| Finding | Action | Risk of ciTBI |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| GCS ≤14 OR altered mental status OR palpable skull fracture | <span style="color:#f87171">CT</span> | 4.4% |
| Scalp hematoma (non-frontal) OR LOC ≥5 sec OR severe mechanism | Consider CT | 0.9% |
| No concerning findings | <span style="color:#34d399">Observe</span> | <0.02% |

**Children ≥2 years:**

| Finding | Action | Risk of ciTBI |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| GCS ≤14 OR basilar skull fracture OR altered mental status | <span style="color:#f87171">CT</span> | 4.3% |
| Vomiting OR LOC OR severe headache OR severe mechanism | Consider CT | 0.9% |
| No concerning findings | <span style="color:#34d399">Observe</span> | <0.05% |

> **ciTBI = clinically important traumatic brain injury**

### C-Spine Clearance

**PECARN Criteria — obtain imaging if ANY present:**
- Altered mental status
- Focal neurologic deficits
- Complaint of neck pain
- Torticollis
- Substantial injury to torso
- Predisposing condition
- High-risk MVA
- Diving accident

**NEXUS Criteria — imaging if ANY present:**
- Midline cervical tenderness
- Altered level of alertness
- Evidence of intoxication
- Neurologic deficit
- <span style="color:#f87171">Painful distracting injury</span>

### SCIWORA
<span style="color:#22d3ee">Spinal Cord Injury Without Radiographic Abnormality</span>

- 30–40% of children with traumatic myelopathy
- Most common in children ≤8 years
- <span style="color:#60a5fa">MRI should be considered</span>

## III. CONCUSSION

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Definition</span> | Neurometabolic dysfunction (not structural) after head trauma |
| Neuroimaging | Usually <span style="color:#34d399">normal</span> |
| Resolution | Most symptoms resolve in <span style="color:#34d399">1–3 weeks</span> |
| Prolonged symptoms | >3 weeks → specialist referral |

### Concussion Symptoms

| Category | Symptoms |
|----------|----------|
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Physical</span> | Headache, photophobia, phonophobia |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Cognitive</span> | Feeling foggy, fatigue, difficulty focusing, forgetfulness |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Emotional</span> | Irritability, sadness |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Sleep</span> | Drowsiness, sleeping more/less |

### Return-to-Play (6 Steps)

| Step | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 1 | Back to regular activities |
| 2 | Light aerobic activity (5–10 min walking/jogging) |
| 3 | Moderate activity (moderate jogging, weightlifting) |
| 4 | Heavy noncontact activity (sprinting, drills) |
| 5 | Full contact in controlled practice |
| 6 | Full competition/game play |

> ⚠️ Only progress if <span style="color:#34d399">no new/worsening symptoms</span>. Steps take days to months.

## IV. THORACIC, ABDOMINAL, AND PELVIC TRAUMA

### Life-Threatening Findings

| Condition | Signs | <span style="color:#f87171">Emergent Treatment</span> |
|-----------|-------|----------------------------------|
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Tension pneumothorax</span> | Respiratory distress, unequal breath sounds, hypotension | Immediate needle decompression, chest tube |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Massive hemothorax</span> | Hypotension, tachycardia, dullness | Chest tube, volume resuscitation |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Cardiac tamponade</span> | Muffled heart sounds, hypotension, pulsus paradoxus | Pericardiocentesis |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Aortic dissection</span> | Severe chest/abdominal pain, pulse differential | Stable: β-blocker; Unstable: OR |

### Rib Fractures & Flail Chest

| Finding | Implication |
|---------|-------------|
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Multiple rib fractures</span> in <3 years | <span style="color:#f87171">Consider child abuse</span> |
| Flail chest | Intubate + positive pressure ventilation |
| Pulmonary contusion WITHOUT rib fracture | More common in children (compliant chest wall) |

### Seatbelt Sign

<span style="color:#f87171">Bruising in seatbelt distribution</span> — sensitivity 70.6%, specificity 82.4% for surgical abdominal injury

### PECARN Blunt Abdominal Trauma Rule

**Very low risk for IAI (99% sensitivity) — ALL must be absent:**
- No abdominal wall trauma / seatbelt sign
- GCS >13
- No abdominal tenderness
- No thoracic wall trauma
- No abdominal pain
- No decreased breath sounds
- No vomiting

### FAST Exam

| Result | Interpretation |
|--------|----------------|
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Positive</span> | IAI likely → CT warranted |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Negative</span> | Cannot rule out → CT or serial FAST |

> Pooled sensitivity: <span style="color:#34d399">35%</span>, Specificity: <span style="color:#34d399">96%</span>

## V. LIMB AND LONG BONE TRAUMA

### Compartment Syndrome — The 5 P's

| Sign | Note |
|------|------|
| <span style="color:#f87171">Pain</span> | Especially with passive extension, out of proportion |
| <span style="color:#f87171">Pallor</span> | — |
| <span style="color:#f87171">Paresthesias</span> | — |
| <span style="color:#f87171">Paresis</span> | — |
| <span style="color:#f87171">Pulselessness</span> | Late sign — use Doppler |

### Pediatric Fracture Types

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Salter-Harris</span> | Fractures involving growth plates |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Torus/Buckle</span> | Buckled cortex — compression injury |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Greenstick</span> | Fracture one side, cortex intact other side |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Bowing</span> | Plastic deformity without fracture line |

### Urgent Orthopedic Consultation

<span style="color:#f87171">⚠️</span> Open fractures, unacceptable displacement, neurovascular compromise, growth plate injuries, displaced long bone fractures, pelvic fractures, spinal fractures

### Common Pediatric Orthopedic Injuries

**TABLE 2.2 — ED MANAGEMENT**

| Injury | ED Management | Follow-Up |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| Clavicle fracture (nondisplaced) | Sling | PCP in 2 weeks |
| AC joint separation | Sling | Ortho in 1 week |
| Proximal humerus fracture | Sling | Ortho in 1 week |
| Distal radius/ulna fracture | Volar splint | Ortho in 1 week |
| Salter-Harris Type 1 — distal radius | Volar splint | Ortho in 1 week |
| Distal radius buckle fracture | Volar splint | Ortho in 1 week |
| Phalanx fracture | Splint/buddy tape | PCP in 2 weeks |

## VI. DENTAL TRAUMA

### Primary vs Permanent Teeth

| Feature | Primary | Permanent |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Age</span> | 6 months – 3 years | 6–21 years |
| <span style="color:#60a5fa">Roots</span> | Shorter | Longer |
| Biting surface | Smooth | Ridged |

### Avulsion

| Tooth Type | Action |
|------------|--------|
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Primary</span> | <span style="color:#f87171">Do NOT reimplant</span> — outpatient dental follow-up |
| <span style="color:#22d3ee">Permanent</span> | <span style="color:#f87171">EMERGENCY</span> — reimplant within 60 minutes |

> **Storage media (if reimplantation not possible):** Milk > Hank's balanced salt solution > saliva > saline

### Tooth Fracture Types

| Type | Follow-Up |
|------|-----------|
| Enamel fracture | Dental evaluation for possible bonding |
| Enamel-dentin fracture | Dental evaluation in 48–72 hr |
| Enamel-dentin-pulp fracture | <span style="color:#f87171">Immediate dental evaluation</span> |
| Alveolar ridge fracture | <span style="color:#f87171">Emergent dental evaluation</span> |

## VII. OPHTHALMOLOGIC TRAUMA

### Chemical Eye Injury

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | <span style="color:#f87171">IMMEDIATE irrigation</span> with water/saline/LR |
| 2 | Call Poison Control: <span style="color:#60a5fa">1-800-222-1222</span> |
| 3 | Determine acid (<7) or alkali (>7.4) — <span style="color:#f87171">Alkali more damaging!</span> |
| 4 | Continue irrigation ≥20 minutes or until pH <span style="color:#34d399">7.0–7.4</span> |

### Ruptured Globe

<span style="color:#f87171">⚠️ EMERGENT ophthalmology consultation</span>

- Stop exam, place <span style="color:#22d3ee">rigid eye shield</span>
- Do NOT instill eye drops
- Elevate HOB
- Control symptoms (antiemetics, pain control)
- Antibiotics

### Corneal Abrasion

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Symptoms | Red eye, tearing, intense pain, photophobia, FB sensation |
| Diagnosis | Fluorescein staining with Wood's lamp |
| Treatment | Artificial tears, antibiotic ointment, analgesics |

## VIII. ANIMAL BITES

### Antibiotic Prophylaxis

| Indication | Treatment |
|------------|-----------|
| <span style="color:#fb923c">Animal bite</span> | <span style="color:#fb923c">Amoxicillin/clavulanate</span> 5 days |
| <span style="color:#fb923c">Human bite</span> | <span style="color:#fb923c">Amoxicillin/clavulanate</span> 5 days |
| Penicillin allergy | TMP/SMX + clindamycin |

> <span style="color:#60a5fa">Dog bite infection median:</span> 24 hours | <span style="color:#60a5fa">Cat bite infection median:</span> 12 hours

### Closure Decision

| Location | Recommendation |
|----------|---------------|
| Face | Primary closure acceptable (<24 hr) |
| Other areas | Avoid primary closure (<12 hr) |
| All | Do NOT close with tissue adhesive |

## IX. BURNS

### Burn Classification

**TABLE 2.5 — BURN DEPTH**

| Depth | Layer | Clinical Findings |
|-------|-------|------------------|
| <span style="color:#34d399">Superficial</span> | Epidermis | Dry, painful, erythematous (like sunburn) |
| <span style="color:#fbbf24">Partial thickness</span> | Dermis | Moist, painful, blisters, blanching |
| <span style="color:#f87171">Full thickness</span> | Subcutaneous/fascia/muscle/bone | Pale, charred, waxy, leathery, <span style="color:#f87171">insensate</span> |

### Parkland Formula

<span style="color:#60a5fa">4 mL/kg × %TBSA burned</span> over first 24 hours

- First half: first 8 hours
- Second half: next 16 hours

> <span style="color:#60a5fa">Goal urine output:</span> 0.5–1 mL/kg/hr

### TBSA Estimation

- <span style="color:#60a5fa">Patient's palm + adducted fingers ≈ 1% TBSA</span>
- Use modified Lund-Browder chart for children

### Fluid Resuscitation by TBSA

| TBSA | Action |
|------|--------|
| <15% | Consider IV fluids based on clinical status |
| ≥15% | LR or NS per Parkland formula |

### Burn Center Transfer Indications

<span style="color:#f87171">⚠️ Partial-thickness ≥10% TBSA | Full-thickness any | Face/hands/feet/genitalia | Electrical/chemical | Inhalation | Burns + trauma</span>

## X. NONACCIDENTIC TRAUMA (CHILD ABUSE)

### Red Flags in History

| Red Flag | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| Inconsistent history | Does not match injury pattern/severity |
| Inconsistent with development | Child cannot yet walk but has fall injury |
| Vague/changing explanations | Different stories from different people |
| Delay in presentation | — |

### Physical Exam Red Flags

| Finding | Pattern |
|---------|---------|
| <span style="color:#f87171">Bruises</span> | Protected areas, multiple, various healing stages, unusual locations |
| <span style="color:#f87171">Burns</span> | Sharply demarcated edges, object shapes, cigarette burns, stocking/glove distribution |
| <span style="color:#f87171">Other</span> | Frenulum tears, loop marks, bites |

### Abusive Fractures (High Specificity)

- <span style="color:#f87171">Posterior rib fractures</span>
- <span style="color:#f87171">Bucket handle / metaphyseal corner fractures</span>
- Spine and scapula fractures
- Skull fractures (with other findings)

### Imaging

| Age | Study |
|-----|-------|
| <2 years | <span style="color:#60a5fa">Full skeletal survey</span> |
| 2–5 years | Clinical judgment |
| >5 years | Targeted imaging |

### Reporting

<span style="color:#f87171">⚠️ ALL healthcare providers are mandated by law to report suspected child maltreatment.</span>

## XI. SEXUAL ABUSE

### Indications for Urgent Evaluation

- Abuse within <span style="color:#60a5fa">72–96 hours</span>
- Genital/anal injuries requiring treatment
- Obvious forensic evidence
- Danger of continued abuse
- Suicidal/homicidal ideation

### STI Testing in Sexual Abuse

| Population | Testing |
|-----------|---------|
| Adolescents | Recommended for all |
| Prepubertal | Consider if: penetration, stranger abuse, known infected perpetrator, sibling with STI, signs/symptoms |
